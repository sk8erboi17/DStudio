Part I

The usefulness of empty space

Mara Ellis

An unfilled margin is not wasted paper. It is where a reader pauses, notices, and makes a connection that the writer could not make for them.1

Interfaces forget this. Every gap is treated as a vacancy to be filled with another prompt, another card, another reason to stay. Yet the pause before a decision is part of the decision; remove it and people do not decide faster, they decide worse.

Space is part of the explanation, not what is left over after it.

Put related things together and leave a deliberate interval before the next task. Let a column end. Let a page say it is finished.2 The reader will supply the rest, and they will remember it because the thought was theirs.

Notes

  1. 1Marginal notes record where an idea came from. They are the opposite of decoration: they make the argument checkable.
  2. 2A finished page is a courtesy. Endless feeds are a design decision, not a law of nature.
